LightSpeedUI separates concerns across four distinct levels so you only adopt as much opinion as your Astro project needs.

## Dependency Architecture

```text
Level 3: Landing (@lsui/landing)  ──▶  Blocks, CMS sections & templates
   │
   ▼  imports
Level 2: Liftoff (@lsui/liftoff)  ──▶  Themed copy-paste .astro components + @lsui/themes tokens
   │
   ▼  imports
Level 1: Origin  (@lsui/origin)   ──▶  Unstyled .astro primitives + headless behaviors + ariaNotify()


Level 4: Apogee  (@lsui/apogee)   ──▶  Independent rich experiences (Tours, Kanban, Spotlight)
                                       (consumes @lsui/origin behaviors without coupling to Landing)
```

## Choosing Your Entry Point

- Reach for **[Origin](/origin/)** when your team has a bespoke CSS system and wants pure Astro accessibility, keyboard navigation, focus management, and `ariaNotify()` announcements with zero visual CSS.
- Reach for **[Liftoff](/liftoff/)** when you want editable, token-themed `.astro` UI, content, and docs components (`Nordic`, `Editorial`, or your own theme) ready to drop into an app or publication.
- Reach for **[Landing](/landing/)** when you want complete marketing heroes, pricing tables, blog grids, and EmDash CMS page templates assembled from `Liftoff` components.
- Reach for **[Apogee](/apogee/)** when you want standalone interactive capabilities like a guided `<Tour />` or multi-column Kanban board.
